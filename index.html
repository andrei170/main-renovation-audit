First — What You Have That Most Roofers Do Not
This is a better foundation than it looks
  • Proper RoofingContractor structured data. Most roofing sites have none. Yours declares your name, phone, email, logo, social profiles and five service types.
  • Tappable phone numbers on every service page — three on commercial, four on residential.
  • Written meta descriptions on the home, commercial and residential pages.
  • A dedicated estimate page with a form on it. A separate conversion page is the right instinct.
  • Real commercial credentials — Conklin training in spray polyurethane foam, roof coatings and single-ply membrane, plus GAF certification on the residential side, and an A+ BBB rating.
  • Insurance-claim work is already one of your declared services — which in Georgia, with its hail and storm seasons, is the highest-value thing on your list.

Nothing here needs to be thrown away. Everything below is about pointing it at the right map.

Finding 1 — You Want To Buy Google Ads. Nothing On Your Site Is Measured.
Tracking installed on mainrenovationllc.com today: Google Analytics 4 not found Google Tag Manager not found Google Ads conversion tag not found Meta pixel not found Call tracking not found Microsoft Clarity not found Nothing. Not one tag on any page.
This is not a small thing — it is the difference between advertising and donating

Modern Google Ads bidding works by learning which clicks turn into customers. You give it a conversion signal — a form submitted, a call placed — and it spends your budget chasing more people who look like the ones who converted. That learning is the product.

With no conversion tag there is no signal. Google can only optimise toward clicks, because clicks are the only thing it can see. You would be paying for traffic and hoping, and at the end of the month nobody could tell you which keyword produced a roof and which produced nothing.

There is a second cost that is easy to miss. Most roofing enquiries are phone calls, not forms. Without call tracking, even a perfectly-run campaign would look like it was failing, because the conversions would be happening on your phone where no system can count them.

This is the first thing to fix and it is not a big job. It is tags, not a rebuild, and it must be done before a single dollar goes into Google Ads — not after.

We checked the served HTML of all four content pages for GA4, Tag Manager, Google Ads, Meta, CallRail and Clarity. A static read cannot prove absence with total certainty, but with no tag-manager container present there is nowhere for a tag to hide, and whoever built the site can confirm it in a minute.

Finding 2 — Your Website Is Fighting Your Own Business Rule
From your own structured data: "areaServed": { "@type": "State", "name": "Georgia" <-- the entire state } "address": { "addressRegion": "GA", "addressCountry": "US" no street, no city, no postal code } What you told Ramses you want: Jobs 30 to 40 minutes from base.
Two separate problems, and they compound

The first is reach. You are telling Google you serve everywhere from Chattanooga to the Florida line. Google will happily show you to somebody four hours away, and you will pay for that click, qualify the lead, and turn the job down. In paid search you pay for the wrong enquiry exactly as much as the right one.

The second is placement. Your address block has no city and no street. Local search ranking leans heavily on where a business actually is. A contractor with no city in their data is difficult for Google to place anywhere in particular — so it places them weakly everywhere, which is the worst outcome for somebody who wants to dominate a tight radius.

Your radius rule is not a limitation. It is a strategy, and a good one. A 30-minute service area is cheaper to advertise in, cheaper to rank in, and far more profitable to run, because your crews are not spending two hours a day in traffic. The site simply does not know about it.

Finding 3 — You Market Atlanta And Hide Loganville
Town names in the visible copy across your whole site: Atlanta 12 mentions Loganville 1 mention (one line, on the residential page) Every other town you could reach: 0 Loganville appears in your meta descriptions and one title tag — and almost nowhere a customer can read it.
Atlanta is probably outside the radius you just described

You are based in Loganville, in Walton County. Downtown Atlanta is around 35 miles west — which in Atlanta traffic is routinely an hour each way, well beyond the 30 to 40 minutes you set as your rule.

Atlanta is also one of the most expensive roofing markets in the South to advertise in. Every national brand, every storm-chaser and every private-equity-backed roofer bids on Atlanta. It is the most costly ground you could possibly have chosen, and it is ground you have said you do not want the work from.

Meanwhile the towns inside your radius have almost no serious competition, because everyone else is also pointing at Atlanta.

The towns to build for — for you to confirm, not us: Snellville, Monroe, Grayson, Dacula, Lawrenceville, Lilburn, Conyers, Covington, Stone Mountain, Lithonia, Between and Walnut Grove. You know your real drive times better than any map does, and the list we build should be yours, not ours.

Finding 4 — Four Pages Of Content, And The Commercial One Is 212 Words
Every page you publish: / Home 592 words 1 form /residential-roofing.html Residential 528 words 1 form /commercial-roofing.html Commercial 212 words 0 forms /estimate.html Estimate 180 words 1 form /privacy-policy.html Legal /terms.html Legal Location pages: none Storm / insurance claim page: none Roof repair page: none
The gaps are all on your highest-value services
  • Commercial is 212 words with no enquiry form on it. You are Conklin-trained in three commercial systems — that is a genuine specialism most residential roofers cannot offer, and it gets a third of a page and no way to enquire.
  • Insurance-claim roofing is in your structured data as a service and has no page. In a hail state, “will my insurance cover this” is one of the most valuable questions a roofer can answer, and it is where the trust is won.
  • Roof repair has no page, despite being listed as a service. Repair searches are how you get in front of somebody two years before they need a replacement.
  • Your estimate page has no meta description. That is the page you want people landing on from an ad.

What We Would Do, In Order
  • Install conversion tracking and call tracking first. GA4, a Google Ads conversion action, and call tracking so phone enquiries are counted. Before any ad spend, not after. Nothing else on this list can be judged without it.
  • Fix the service area in your data. Replace “State of Georgia” with the actual towns you will drive to, and put your city into your address markup so Google can place you.
  • Build a page for each town in your radius — your list, confirmed by you, starting with the closest and the wealthiest housing stock rather than the biggest.
  • Write the three missing service pages: roof repair, storm damage and insurance claims. The claims page is the most valuable page you do not currently have.
  • Fix the commercial page — put the Conklin systems, the specification detail and an enquiry form on it. It is your highest-margin work and your thinnest page.
  • Then run Google Ads, on a tight radius around your own towns, with the tracking already live so you can see cost per booked job from week one rather than guessing in month three.

You already decided you want tight, local, profitable work. Your website never got told.

Put the meter on, put your own towns in, and stop paying Atlanta prices for jobs you would turn down anyway.
